The Editorial Architect — a system designed to be read, not just seen.

Version 3.0 rebalances the brand. The architectural rigor stays — systems over hacks, structure underneath everything — but the voice is now editorial: warm paper, a classical serif, and a single cultural accent where cold corporate blue-and-orange used to live.

What changed from v2.0 → v3.0

Added Fraunces as display serif — brings editorial weight, cultural warmth, and opens up italic expression for emphasis words.
Added Paper / Bone warm cream backgrounds as the default for editorial contexts (Instagram carousels, long-form, educational content). Dark mode becomes one of two official modes, not the only mode.
Replaced Electric Orange (#F97316) → Terracotta (#B8553A). Same function (accent, emphasis, the dot in the logo), but now carries cultural warmth instead of corporate-tech signaling.
Refined Blueprint Blue (#3B82F6) → Signal Blue (#2B5CE6). A deeper, more confident blue that reads as considered rather than generic SaaS.
Replaced Space Grotesk → Inter for UI and labels. Geometric modern sans stays, but Inter is more neutral — it lets Fraunces carry the brand personality.
Kept The architectural DNA: grid overlays (now masked for subtlety), monospace for technical/data, the dot-as-accent in the logo. Your IT background is real — this is not the brand to abandon structure.
01 / Foundation

Brand principles

Three tensions held in balance. Every design decision should honor all three — if a piece leans too far in one direction, it's off-brand.

Structured, but human. There is always a system underneath — but the person delivering it is warm, bilingual, and reads books. The design should signal both.

Technical, but editorial. Monospace and grids exist because the work is real. Serif headlines and cream paper exist because the audience isn't engineers — they're entrepreneurs who deserve a brand that respects their intelligence without performing tech-bro signaling.

AI-native, but human-guided. The tagline is the product. The visual system must reinforce both halves equally.

The 80/15/5 rule. 80% of any piece is Paper or Deep Ink. 15% is type in ink/paper/slate. 5% is Signal Blue for structure and Terracotta for emphasis. If you're above 5% on the accents, you're shouting.

A serif-led system

Fraunces carries the brand voice. Inter does the quiet work of UI and body. JetBrains Mono keeps the technical DNA without making everything look like a terminal.

Rule of emphasis: In any headline, italicize one word. That word is almost always the concept you want to own. "La IA no te reemplaza." "Marketing con sistema." This is the single most powerful move in the system — it replaces the need for bold, color changes, or size jumps.

Fraunces is the flagship — get it from Google Fonts. Inter is the workhorse — rsms.me/inter. JetBrains Mono replaces Geist Mono — it's free-license and ships on more systems.

04 / Modes

Two official modes

Not "dark mode and a light mode afterthought." Two first-class presentations of the brand, each with a distinct use case.

◐ Light — "Paper"
Para el contenido editorial
Instagram carousels, LinkedIn long-form, sales pages, newsletters, anything that needs to feel warm, considered, and readable in a lifestyle feed.
Default mode for creator content
◑ Dark — "Ink"
Para el producto técnico
Landing pages, the website, dashboards, documentation, anything that needs to signal technical depth and product-grade thinking.
Default mode for product surfaces
05 / Logo

The wordmark

The brand name set in Fraunces italic, with a terracotta dot as the pivot. No brackets, no blueprint crop marks, no bespoke "A" monogram. The wordmark is the logo. The italic is the personality. The dot is the signature.

Monogram · Avatar / favicon

Logo rules: The dot is always Terracotta (#B8553A). "Abdias" is always roman. "Marketing" is always italic. Clear space around the mark equals the x-height of the lowercase letters. Minimum size: 100px wide for legibility of the italic.

Why no bespoke mark? Because your brand is an editorial voice — the typography is the identity. A monogram in a box would fight the serif. Trust the wordmark to do the work.

07 / Guardrails

Do's & don'ts

Do

  • Lead with Fraunces for headlines — one italic emphasis word per headline
  • Default to Paper backgrounds for editorial content
  • Default to Ink backgrounds for product and technical surfaces
  • Use Terracotta like salt — sparingly, with intention
  • Keep monospace for actual technical content (code, data, labels, timestamps)
  • Maintain wide margins and generous leading
  • Let one slide per carousel break the rhythm (the "ink" slide)
  • Sign off with the dot-logo in footers and captions

Don't

  • Don't use Fraunces for UI labels or small body text — it loses its character under 16px
  • Don't use Terracotta as a fill color or background mass
  • Don't use pure black (#000) or pure white (#FFF) anywhere in the system
  • Don't add bright neon, saturated gradients, or glow effects
  • Don't italicize more than one word per headline
  • Don't default to Dark mode for Instagram — it disappears in the feed
  • Don't use emoji as primary visual language — ever
  • Don't mix the Editorial Architect system with "guru" aesthetics (cash, cars, hype)
